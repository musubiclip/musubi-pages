# Instagram clip page network: spam, ban and copyright risk research

Researched 2026-10-22. Marks: **[official]** = Meta/Instagram's own page or docs; **[press]** = journalism or a vendor reporting what Meta said; **[folklore]** = growth blogs, forums, vendors selling tools. Where a Meta page shows no date, the date given is when it was accessed.

## 1. Multiple accounts, networks, inauthentic behaviour, linked accounts

- **No official cap on how many Instagram accounts one person or business may own.** The Help Center says only that you can have **up to 10 accounts logged in at once** and switch between them. The old "5 accounts" figure is out of date. The real-name and one-account rules in Meta's identity policy are written for **Facebook** profiles, not Instagram. [official] https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/1696686240613595 (accessed 2026-10-22); https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/authentic-identity-representation/ (change log Sep 11, 2026)
- **Instagram's own Community Guidelines URL now redirects to Meta's Community Standards**, which "outline what is and isn't allowed on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and Threads". The Meta policies below therefore govern Instagram. [official] https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- **Inauthentic Behaviour (current text):** a network of inauthentic assets "controlled by the same individual or individuals, with the goal of deceiving Meta or our community or to evade enforcement". The policy prohibits using inauthentic assets to "deceive Meta or our users about the identity, or origin of an audience or the entity that they represent" or "to evade enforcement". **Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour** is the sophisticated kind, "where false identities are central to the operation and operators use adversarial methods to evade detection or appear authentic". Owning several real, branded pages is not prohibited in itself. Hiding common control so the pages look independent is what moves a network towards this policy. [official] https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/inauthentic-behavior (change log Dec 11, 2025)
- **Linked accounts can be disabled together (Account Integrity policy).** Meta may restrict or disable accounts:
  - "Owned by the same person or entity as an account that has been disabled"
  - "Created or repurposed to evade a previous account or entity removal"
  - with "Close linkage with a network of accounts … that violate or evade our policies"
  - with "Coordination within a network of accounts … that persistently or egregiously violate our policies"

  Creating or using accounts "through automated means, such as scripting" can trigger a request for more information, unless the scripting "occurs through authorized routes". [official] https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/account-integrity/ (change log May 28, 2026)
- **"Accounts created to get around our restrictions" are disabled.** Strikes count across both Facebook and Instagram and expire after one year. Meta may disable an account after five or more strikes, or after one severe violation. [official] https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/disabling-accounts/ (updated Jul 17, 2025); https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/counting-strikes/ (updated Apr 23, 2025)
- **Meta does remove linked accounts in bulk in practice.** It took down about 135,000 Instagram accounts plus 500,000 "linked" Instagram and Facebook accounts in one action, for child safety violations. [press] https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meta-updates-safety-features-for-teens-more-than-600000-accounts-linked-to-predatory-behavior/6345127/ (Jul 2025)
- **Wrongful mass bans in 2025, with whole account families lost.** Tens of thousands of users reported wrongful bans. In one case a business Instagram account was suspended together with every linked Meta account, including Facebook pages and Threads. Meta admitted only a Facebook Groups error. Its appeals are widely described as automated or unanswered. [press] https://capitalfm.africa/instagram-users-angry-and-confused-as-meta-overturns-yet-more-account-bans/ (BBC syndication, Aug 2025); https://www.ctvnews.ca/business/article/we-lost-things-well-never-get-back-sask-business-owners-claim-wrongful-instagram-suspensions-caused-by-ai/ (2025)
- **The Oversight Board (June 2026) found Meta's disabling rules unclear.** Meta "does not publicly set out all the restrictions it can apply to Instagram accounts". Meta told the Board it does **not** temporarily suspend Instagram accounts for violations, so the step after a warning is often a full disable. [official, Oversight Board] https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-meta-oversight-board-users-informed-government-requests-lead-account-ban/ (Jun 2026)
- **Running many accounts under one business is supported officially.** A Meta business portfolio and Business Suite hold multiple Instagram professional accounts. The Instagram API's **Standard Access** exists precisely for "your Instagram professional account or an account you manage", with no App Review needed. A cap of 25 accounts per portfolio is widely quoted but unverified. [official] https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/app-review (Jun 2026); 25 cap [folklore] https://blog.send.win/meta-business-suite-manage-multiple-instagram-accounts-limit-multi-account-management-guide-2026/

## 2. What triggers checkpoints, phone checks, action blocks and disables

- **Spam policy (official):** prohibits "Posting, sharing, engaging with content or **creating accounts** … at very high frequencies", whether done manually or automatically. Meta may also restrict "accounts that are acting at lower frequencies when other indicators of Spam … are present". The policy publishes no numbers. [official] https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/ (change log Jun 26, 2024)
- **Identity checks (official, 2020; still the mechanism).** Instagram asks the account owner to confirm their identity when it sees "a pattern of potential inauthentic behavior": possible coordinated inauthentic behaviour, a majority of followers in another country, or "signs of automation". If the owner does not confirm, the content gets "reduced distribution, or the account may be disabled". [official] https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/introducing-new-authenticity-measures-on-instagram/ (Aug 13, 2020)
- **Video selfie check for new accounts suspected of being bots.** Instagram asks the owner of a suspicious new account to record a video selfie. A person must do this, and the face is the same across every account that person runs. [press] https://www.androidcentral.com/instagram-video-selfie-for-identity-verification (2021)
- **Scraping and automation restrictions.** An account "may have been restricted and flagged for further review" if it automates access or collects information without permission, if its password was given to a third party app, or if it used "an app or service to interact with Instagram in unauthorised ways". The Terms of Use forbid trying to "create accounts or access or collect information in unauthorized ways" (since Nov 2021). [official] https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/740480200552298 (accessed 2026-10-22); https://opentermsarchive.org/en/memos/instagram-blocks-data-retrieval-and-clarifies-what-information-it-retrieves/
- **Automated likes, comments and follows** from apps outside Instagram "go against" the rules, and Instagram removes that activity. [official] https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/572730176521116 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- **Follow and unfollow numbers are all folklore.** Meta publishes no daily limits; only the 7,500 total following cap is widely cited as official. Vendor blogs disagree: 20 to 100 follows a day for new accounts, 30 to 50 unfollows a day, 7 to 13 follows an hour, blocks lasting hours to 5 days. They agree that bursts and churn patterns matter more than the daily total. [folklore] https://www.unfollr.com/blog/instagram-follow-unfollow-limits (2026); https://tagembed.com/blog/instagram-restrictions-follow-like-comment/ (2026)
- **How many accounts one phone number can hold: no official answer.** "5 per number" comes from a 2024 Guiding Tech article and similar blogs. One 2026 guide notes that a shared verification number is itself a linking signal. [folklore] https://www.guidingtech.com/why-cant-i-create-an-instagram-account (Jun 2024); https://stackinfluence.com/blog/how-many-instagram-accounts-can-i-have-tips (2026)
- **Same IP, same device, browser sign up, email aliases on one domain: no Meta statement.** Every claim comes from proxy, antidetect and growth vendors: "5 accounts per IP", device fingerprinting, linking by recovery email. Those vendors sell the cure, so their numbers are worthless. Separately, agencies routinely run many brands from one office IP through Business Suite. [folklore] https://geonode.com/blog/what-is-ip-banned-Instagram ; https://malwaretips.com/threads/instagram-account-disabled-twice-what-can-i-do-to-not-to-be-disabled-again-or-risk-being-banned.141994/
- **Indian prepaid numbers are recycled.** Under TRAI rules, a number can be deactivated after 90 days without use (unless the balance is Rs 20 or more) and then reissued to someone else after a 90 day hold. A lapsed shared SIM can therefore end up recovering all 10 accounts for a stranger. [official, TRAI via court record] https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/once-a-mobile-number-is-deactivated-it-is-not-assigned-to-a-new-user-for-90-days-trai-to-supreme-court-241506 (Nov 3, 2023)

## 3. Warming up

- **No Meta or Instagram source mentions warming up an account.** All of the advice comes from vendors and growth sellers. post-bridge, a scheduling vendor, recommends 7 days of scrolling, following and commenting with no posts, and its first ~10 posts from the phone app. A Fiverr seller offers warm-up as a service. None of them cite data. [folklore] https://post-bridge.com/growth-guide/account-warmup (undated); https://www.superscale.ai/learn/the-right-way-to-warm-up-new-tiktok-instagram-accounts
- **What is real underneath:** Meta's systems use account age, behaviour pattern and automation signals (the Spam policy and the 2020 identity checks above). Ordinary human use of a new account looks unlike a bot. A warm-up made of follow and unfollow churn looks *more* like one.
- **Instagram's own advice to new professional accounts** is about content, not warming up:
  - Post original work with no visible watermarks.
  - Check Account Status, which shows removed content, features you cannot use, and recommendation eligibility, with a request for review. "In most cases, you'll get a warning before losing access."
  - Mosseri: no ideal posting frequency; "the more you post, the more people you'll reach". Reels are ranked on watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach.

  [official] https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/539126347315373 (accessed 2026-10-22); https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality (Apr 30, 2024); Mosseri frequency [press] https://routenote.com/blog/how-often-you-should-post-on-instagram/
- **Trial Reels** (shown to non-followers first) can be published through the API (`trial_params`). It reportedly needs a public account with 1,000 or more followers, so a new page may not qualify yet. [official API] https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/ ; follower threshold [press] https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm (2026)

## 4. Does posting through the official API or a scheduler hurt reach or raise spam risk?

- **Mosseri says scheduling does not affect reach.** He said scheduled posts "will not affect your reach one way or the other" (Stories Q&A, Mar 29, 2025), repeated it in March 2026, and in August 2026 said of Reels: "There's no difference in terms of your reach. You can schedule a Reel, or you can post it yourself." [press, quoting Mosseri] https://www.kontentino.com/blog/does-scheduling-instagram-posts-hurt-reach/ (Oct 19, 2026); https://planoly.com/blog/debunking-common-instagram-myths (Aug 2025)
- **The Content Publishing API is the sanctioned route**: Instagram API with Instagram Login, `instagram_business_basic` plus `instagram_business_content_publish`, on a professional account. The limit is **100 API-published posts per 24 hours** per account, so 1 to 3 a day is far below it. Standard Access with no App Review covers accounts you own or manage. This is the "authorized route" the Account Integrity policy allows. [official] https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/ (accessed 2026-10-22); https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/app-review (Jun 2026)
- **What the API does not include:** Instagram's music library, filters and shopping tags. [official] same content publishing page.
- **The API can check copyright before you post.** `copyright_check_status` on an unpublished container returns `matches_found`, the matched audio or video segments, and the owner's policy (`BLOCK` or `MUTE` by country; the sample response has UMG blocking in India). `copyright_check_information` does the same for published media. The guide is filed under the Facebook Login variant of the API, so test it with Instagram Login tokens. [official] https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/copyright-detection (sample uses v26.0)
- **"First posts from the mobile app" is folklore**, even when a scheduling vendor says it. [folklore] https://post-bridge.com/growth-guide/account-warmup

## 5. Copyright for clip pages

- **Repeat infringer policy: no published strike count.** Meta "disable[s] the accounts of repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances". The policy covers Instagram (copyright, trademark, counterfeit). Lesser steps include "prohibiting the posting of content for a set period of time". Content restored after an appeal, or after the rights owner retracts the report, is "take[n] into account". Copyright follows its own repeat infringer track, separate from the Community Standards strike ladder. [official] https://transparency.meta.com/data/intellectual-property/protecting-intellectual-property-rights/ (accessed 2026-10-22); https://www.meta.com/help/policies/2202628709913826/ (updated ~Oct 2026)
- **Real case: warnings came before the disable.** Catholic Connect (273k followers) was disabled after "multiple" reports and an earlier warning. [press] https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2021/03/01/catholic-connect-suspended-from-instagram-for-apparent-copyright-infringement/ (Mar 1, 2021)
- **Credit and disclaimers do not protect you.** Instagram lists, as things that do *not* prevent infringement: "Gave credit to the copyright owner", "Included a disclaimer that you didn't intend to infringe", "Bought or downloaded the content", "Think that the use is a fair use". It advises: "get written permission from the author of the work before posting". [official] https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/354736791367645 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- **Documenting permission (practical, from the above):**
  - Get a written licence from each source owner, by email or a signed letter, that:
    - names the channel or videos,
    - grants clipping, editing and posting on Instagram,
    - names our 10 handles,
    - says whether monetisation is allowed,
    - states a term and how it can be revoked.
  - Keep it on file, because a counter notice or appeal needs it.
  - Rights Manager lets rights holders "authorize certain people or Pages or Instagram accounts to have permission to post videos matching your content". Ask any source that uses Rights Manager to add our handles to that list.

  [official, older doc] https://tubefilter.com/2018/02/09/facebook-expands-copyright-protection-instagram-videos/ ; https://www.rightsmanager.fb.com
- **Rights Manager and Content Protection catch clips automatically.** Rights holders upload reference files and choose to block (only the uploader can still see the video), monitor, claim ad earnings or report as an IP violation. Facebook's **Content Protection** (Nov 2025) finds full *or partial* matches of creators' reels across Facebook and Instagram, and lets the creator track the copy with an "original by" label, block it, or allowlist accounts. A block from Content Protection does not discipline the reposting account, but a formal IP *report* feeds the repeat infringer policy. [official] https://transparency.meta.com/data/intellectual-property/protecting-intellectual-property-rights/ ; [press] https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/17/meta-releases-a-new-tool-to-protect-reels-creators-from-having-their-work-stolen (Nov 17, 2025)
- **Disputing a takedown:**
  - Content removed under the US DMCA: file a counter notice through the Instagram copyright appeal form. Meta forwards it to the reporter and restores the content after 10 to 14 business days unless the reporter files a lawsuit.
  - Otherwise: follow the appeal instructions in the removal notice, or ask the rights owner to retract the report.

  [official] https://help.instagram.com/contact/3504278336481044 ; https://www.meta.com/help/policies/3234337743488413/
- **Music inside the source video.** Meta's Music Guidelines: "Use of music for commercial or non-personal purposes … is prohibited" without your own licence. Content "may be blocked, muted or removed". "The greater the density of music in content, the more likely it may be limited". There is no safe number of seconds. Some business accounts lose access to the licensed music library and get only the royalty-free Meta Sound Collection. Muting can apply by country. A podcaster's permission does **not** cover a label's track in their intro or background. [official] https://www.facebook.com/legal/music_guidelines (effective Mar 26, 2024); https://help.instagram.com/402084904469945 ; no safe length [folklore/legal blog] https://www.foximusic.com/blog/instagram-reels-music-copyright-legal-guide/ (2026)
- **Originality is a separate gate from copyright, and it decides reach.**
  - Accounts that "primarily re-upload others' work" are **not eligible for recommendations**: Reels since Apr 2024, photos and carousels since Apr 30, 2026.
  - Low-effort edits do not count: "borders, watermarks, speed changes, just crediting the original creator", "simply adding a different caption".
  - Edits that count: on-screen text "that does more than describe what's happening", narration, voiceover, commentary, re-contextualising graphics.
  - Eligibility returns when "most of your recently posted reels, photos, and carousels are considered original in a 30-day period", calculated on a rolling basis and shown in Account Status.
  - A licence from the creator does not by itself make a clip original. The guidelines are "separate from" IP policy.
  - Instagram's 2024 change also **replaces a copy with the original** in recommendations, and labels the copy with a link to the creator. If the podcaster posts the same clip on Instagram, our version loses.
  - Recommendation eligibility also requires "no visible watermarks".
  - The "10 reposts in 30 days" threshold appears only in trade press and is unconfirmed.

  [official] https://creators.instagram.com/original-content-guidelines/ (accessed 2026-10-22); https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram (Apr 30, 2026); https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality (Apr 30, 2024); [press] https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator/
- **Recommendation guidelines exclude "content that is largely re-created from another source with little added value"**, and also accounts that "repeatedly use deceptive practices to build followers, such as buying likes". [official] https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/313829416281232 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- **Facebook side, if the reels are cross-posted there.** Accounts that repeatedly reuse others' content lose monetisation and get reduced distribution, and duplicate videos are demoted. In H1 2025 Meta acted on about 500k accounts for "spammy behavior or fake engagement". [press] https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content (Jul 14, 2025)

## 6. Engagement pods, follow for follow, bought followers, cross promotion

- **Buying, selling or exchanging engagement is prohibited.** The Spam policy bans "selling, buying, or exchanging for engagement, such as likes, shares, views, follows". Engagement pods and follow for follow are exchanges of engagement. [official] https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/
- **Bought engagement costs recommendations for the whole account.** An account that "repeatedly use[s] deceptive practices to build followers, such as buying likes" is not recommended. Instagram removes automated likes and follows, and follower counts drop. [official] https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/313829416281232 ; https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/572730176521116
- **Pods have been enforced against before.** Facebook removed 10 large pod groups, and Instagram said they violated its terms. [press] https://petapixel.com/2018/05/15/facebook-nukes-10-major-groups-used-to-game-instagrams-algorithm (May 15, 2018). Detection of manual pods is weak, per academic work, so most pod risk talk is [folklore] https://circleboom.com/blog/instagram-pod
- **Cross promotion between your own pages is explicitly allowed.** The Spam policy's exceptions include "Cross promotion that is not triggered by payment to a third party". Tagging sister pages and Collab posts between your own accounts are therefore permitted. No source restricts same-owner Collabs; that is [folklore by absence]. [official] https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/
- **Where cross promotion becomes a problem:**
  - Sister pages liking or commenting on each other to inflate counts, or presenting themselves as independent fans, falls under "deceive … about the identity, or origin of an audience" (Inauthentic Behaviour) and engagement inflation.
  - Posting the same clip on several of the pages runs into duplicate demotion and originality.
  - Meta's separate "Inauthentic Sharing" distribution rule, which covered many seemingly independent pages sharing the same material, was **removed** from the Content Distribution Guidelines in July 2025. The Community Standards above still apply.

  [official] https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/cdgs-changes-corrections/ (Jul 2, 2025)

## What this means for the plan

- **Hiding the link between the pages does not work.** Separate Chrome profiles, independent names and email aliases do not separate the 10 pages. They share:
  - the IP address,
  - the SIM,
  - the email domain,
  - one Meta developer app (Standard Access needs each account added to the app),
  - one person's selfie if a video check comes.

  Meta's policy targets *hidden* control used to deceive or evade, not common ownership. Owning them openly, in one business portfolio, is the clean position. Antidetect browsers, proxies or SIM farms would be the adversarial "evade detection" pattern.
- **The 10 pages share one fate.** Under Account Integrity, a disabled page can take its siblings with it ("owned by the same … entity", "close linkage with a network"). Copyright is the likeliest trigger for a disable. Instagram has no temporary suspension step.
- **Follow and unfollow churn is the only part of the warm-up that matches a written Meta rule** (high-frequency engagement, automation signals). It is also the main folklore trigger for action blocks. Drop the unfollows.
- **Create the accounts a few days apart, not all 10 in one sitting.** The Spam policy names "creating accounts … at very high frequencies".
- **One shared prepaid SIM** is a single point of failure: it links all the accounts and can be recycled after 90 days unused. The per-number limit is unknown. Authenticator 2FA plus recovery codes is the right base.
- **Reach risk outweighs ban risk.** Credited, permitted clips still count as unoriginal unless there is real commentary, voiceover or added context. A brand new page that is ineligible for recommendations gets close to zero reach, and if the source posts the same clip on Instagram, theirs replaces ours.
- **Copyright:** get written licences, ask sources to allowlist the handles in Rights Manager, and run `copyright_check_status` before every publish. Cut or replace background music, which the podcaster's permission does not cover.
